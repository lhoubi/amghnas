Tashelhit, Tamazight, Tarifit: Navigating the Amazigh Dialect Continuum

By Anaruz ElhabibUpdated September 20267 min read
ⵜⴰⵛⵍⵃⵉⵜ
At a Glance: The Moroccan Amazigh Continuum
  • Tashelhit (Shilha): Spoken in southern Morocco (Souss-Massa, High Atlas, Anti-Atlas) by 8–10 million people.
  • Central Atlas Tamazight: Middle Atlas mountains (Khenifra, Midelt, Azrou).
  • Tarifit: Spoken across the northern Rif coastal mountains (Al Hoceima, Nador).
  • Writing: Standardized nationally through IRCAM Neo-Tifinagh.

In Morocco, Tamazight flourishes through three major regional varieties that form an interconnected geographic continuum: Tashelhit in the south, Central Atlas Tamazight in the interior mountains, and Tarifit in the northern Rif. While each has distinct lexical nuances and local rhythms, they share an identical grammatical bedrock and rich cultural history.

Tashelhit (Souss & Southern Morocco)

Tashelhit (ⵜⴰⵛⵍⵃⵉⵜ, also written Tachelhit or Shilha) is the most widely spoken Amazigh variety in Morocco and across North Africa, boasting over 8 to 10 million speakers. Its homeland stretches from the peaks of Mount Toubkal in the High Atlas through the fertile Souss Valley (Taroudant, Tiznit, Agadir) down to the Anti-Atlas and the northern edge of the Sahara.

Tashelhit has the oldest and richest written literary tradition of any Berber language. As early as the 16th century, scholars in the Souss wrote extensive religious, medical, and legal treatises in Tashelhit using Arabic script, such as Muhammad Awzal's celebrated poetic masterpiece Al-Hawd. It is also famous for the Rwais — roving troupes of poet-philosophers playing the one-stringed rebab and bronze bell (naqus).

Central Atlas Tamazight (Middle Atlas)

Spoken in the central mountain spine of Morocco — spanning Khenifra, Ifrane, Beni Mellal, and Midelt — this variety is often colloquially called Tamazight in central Morocco. It is known for its energetic Ahidous collective dances and vibrant epic poetry (Tamawayt) sung across the cedar forests.

Tarifit (The Northern Rif Mountains)

Tarifit (ⵜⴰⵔⵉⴼⵉⵜ) is spoken across the northern Mediterranean Rif mountains from Tangier and Tetouan eastward through Al Hoceima and Nador to the Moulouya River. Tarifit features fascinating phonetic developments, such as the vocalization of /l/ into /r/ (e.g., ul "heart" becoming ur) and a large diaspora across the Netherlands, Germany, Belgium, and Spain.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Tashelhit (Shilha)?
Tashelhit is the largest Amazigh variety in Morocco, spoken by 8 to 10 million people primarily in Agadir, the Souss valley, and the High and Anti-Atlas mountains.
Can speakers of Tashelhit and Tarifit understand each other?
While basic vocabulary and grammar are shared, regional accents and slang differ. However, standard Tamazight education and media have made cross-dialect communication increasingly effortless.
